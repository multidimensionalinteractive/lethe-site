01

opening statement

THE SUBJECT OF THE WORK

The true subject of my work is taking the viewer on an inside journey through how I perceive and truly see, in an intuitive sense, the world of the Eastern Front.

I do not do this because of anything likely to be assumed about my reason for doing it.

The Eastern Front is a multifaceted, extremely complex, understudied, underviewed, and undercomprehended section of human history. I believe it possesses incredible value, knowledge, and examples of inherent self-empowerment for viewers, if they are permitted to view it through a lens similar to how I see it.

Of course, the perspective of the viewer will never be precisely mine.

This is my damned attempt to illustrate it.

The work concerns masculinity and vulnerability, destruction and beauty, spiritual ascendance, and what the individual leaves behind after the event. It also concerns the renewal, empowerment, and ennobling that can be created through trauma and conditions of extremity, whether we like it or not.

03

the individual within the event

THE INDIVIDUAL WITHIN THE EVENT

I am drawn to the inner lives of individuals held within an enormous historical event.

I usually know very little about the people I paint. I often do not know their names, whether they lived or died, or anything beyond perhaps a location, date, or uncertain caption.

I cannot completely associate words with why this matters so profoundly to me.

I have felt my whole life that something was pulling me academically and artistically toward these subjects.

I believe greatly, from my heart and in a truthful, non-ignoble way, that there is something valuable and important to humanity within them, especially concerning men as a whole.

I do not invent biographies for them or claim knowledge I do not possess.

04

the photograph and the process

WHAT I SEE WITHIN THE PHOTOGRAPH

When I encounter a photograph, it is not so much an arresting perception as it is that an intuitive cord is struck, and something resonant connects, in an analogous way, to something within me.

I do not know if I can completely put this into words, nor is it something simple to quantify.

At times, the subject seems to ask me, in a metaphysical way, to display them as they would want the world to see them through the ability I have to display them on my canvas.

Then I feel a sensation similar to a bell ringing.

That marks the beginning of this complex and beautiful process of painting.

WHAT PAINTING DOES THAT THE PHOTOGRAPH DOES NOT

When I translate an image through my own weaving onto canvas, it transcribes intuitive and subconscious forces that exist through my perception of the individual within the photograph.

I transfer this in a somewhat automatic, almost metaphysical state.

Perhaps I am opening a portal.

I do not really know.

I transfer what they appear to carry residually through time into the present on my canvas.

It creates an entirely different form of encounter from the photograph.

beauty

THE ROLE OF BEAUTY

Everything I do is intimately connected with beauty.

I think there is extreme, compelling, and profound beauty within this material, which is often associated only with devastation.

Beauty can, from what I have been told, intensify horror, but in my case it restores tenderness, dignity, and humanity.

It invites the viewer into the compulsion to look longer and perhaps unlock something that needs to be let go of, so they can release themselves from their own private fronts and encirclements.

It produces a complicated encounter through Jungian psychology, metaphysical connection, and the whole.

05

the title

YOU ARE STILL INSIDE IT

The title began as a mnemonic idea and what I thought was a good website name, but it also became the larger psychological and philosophical narrative of my work.

What is the front that you are still inside of?

Are you held within an encirclement in your own personal life as an individual?

How can you become aware of that front and attempt to break out of it?

The historical front becomes a parallel for an interior one.

WHAT IS THE FRONT THAT YOU ARE STILL INSIDE OF?

ARE YOU HELD WITHIN AN ENCIRCLEMENT IN YOUR OWN PERSONAL LIFE?

HOW CAN YOU BECOME AWARE OF THAT FRONT AND ATTEMPT TO BREAK OUT OF IT?

06

the moniker

LETHE

Lethe is the moniker I use for this body of work.

It is the name of the river in mythology and also the name of the divinity of oblivion, eternal non-remembrance, and obliteration herself.

Upon entering Hades, there is the choice to partake of the waters of Mnemosyne—eternal memory and recollection—or Lethe, the waters of eternal oblivion and bliss.

I chose Lethe because I look at the Eastern Front through this tension between remembrance and obliteration.

On a somewhat macrocosmic level, I look at the immense detritus and the intimate subjects and knowledge contained within the front through the persona of Lethe herself, as if I were Lethe looking at what remained after the event.

Stalingrad is the extremity of the narrative.

The world existing within that narrative is the Eastern Front itself.

07

illumination

ILLUMINATION

The work is not about glorification, and it is not about condemnation either.

It is about illumination.

War reveals structures that are usually hidden: psychological, social, and existential.

It strips things down.

What I am trying to capture is what remains when those layers are removed.

Every soldier within that conflict, regardless of nation, was a human being first—often conscripted, often without full agency, and operating inside systems far larger than themselves.

To engage with that honestly means holding multiple truths at once: the brutality, the humanity, and the contradictions.

The work is not political or ideological.

It is philosophical, intuitive, metaphysical, and intimate.

Philosophy is more powerful to me than ideology. It concerns the creation of individual ethics: principles for the personal self and on a civic-minded level.

08

research

THE HISTORICAL FOUNDATION

Research is part of everything I do.

Nothing I make at present as an artist would be possible without a lifelong, individually pursued, and academically serious engagement with historical information.

I have been fascinated and compelled by history since I was a toddler.

I could not create or disseminate art concerning this history, nor would I dare make a body of work surrounding it, without a complex, decades-long personal study.

That requires using logic and discerning academic material from media bias, propaganda, and material created for a political purpose.

Without analyzing memoirs, archival material, letters, photographs, interviews, battlefield archaeology, and material culture, one cannot comprehend the dramatic and human reality of the situation.

One may simply reproduce its appearance.

Everything I do on the inner level is created through my comprehension of this material and my constant desire to search for more primary-source, factual, and academic information about the conflict.

10

the artist

THE ARTIST

Alexandria Chanel is a figurative painter and independent historical researcher based on the West Coast.

Her work concerns the world of the Eastern Front, the individual within the event, and what remains after it.

She has been fascinated and compelled by history since childhood. Her practice developed through decades of historical study, archival research, painting, intuition, Jungian psychology, and metaphysical connection.

Her ongoing body of work carries the moniker Lethe and moves between remembrance and obliteration, the enormous historical event and the intimate individual held within it.

The work is not about glorification or condemnation.

It is about illumination.

11

continuing work

THE CONTINUING BODY OF WORK

I have no plans to abandon it.

I have no idea what would be a possible or logical thing to deviate toward.

I have innumerable things to show, share, and look at on a minute level about the front, as well as other facets of the front that are equally important to use in my current series.

I have no intention of putting it down, nor do I lack the fire to continue revealing and sharing this world.

correspondence

ACQUISITIONS, COLLABORATION, AND CORRESPONDENCE

I welcome serious relationships with collectors, benefactors, curators, academics, historians, professors, writers, battlefield archaeologists, post-Soviet civilians, combat veterans, art lovers, military-history enthusiasts, podcasters, debaters, and collaborators.

I am also interested in finding an agent and a committed team capable of helping me disseminate and market the work without simplifying or misrepresenting it.

I feel there is profound material value within the multifaceted and multinarrative nature of my work.

I know I have something, and I want to show and share it with as many people as possible.

Eventually, I also want to give back, especially to combat veterans, in a way that is useful and important.
